## What Is GIMP

GIMP is an open source image editing environment for people who want control over photos, graphics, and digital paintings. Searches such as GIMP photo editing, GIMP image editor, and GIMP graphic design usually point to the same need: a reliable place to open an image, make precise changes, and export a clean result. GIMP meets that need with a long-standing toolset that feels familiar to anyone who has worked with layers, selections, and brushes.

The GIMP image editor approach is modular. Users can keep a simple photo correction session short, or expand into complex compositing with masks, channels, paths, and smart filters. GIMP photo editing is not limited to one style; it supports quick adjustments as well as detailed pixel work. For many users, GIMP graphic design starts with a blank canvas, a few guides, and a clear idea, then grows through experimentation.

GIMP retouching tools are especially useful when an image needs subtle repair rather than a full redesign. Blemishes, dust, scratches, and uneven lighting can be addressed with clone, heal, dodge, burn, and curve adjustments. A GIMP painting workspace also matters for illustrators who prefer a natural brush feel, custom brush packs, and pressure-sensitive strokes. The same environment can handle photo cleanup and painted detail, which keeps creative sessions coherent.

Because GIMP is open source, its development is shaped by a wide community. That means documentation, tutorials, plug-ins, and brush sets are easy to find. Users who search for GIMP photo editing often discover a path that starts with one correction and gradually includes GIMP image editor shortcuts, GIMP graphic design habits, and GIMP retouching tools for repeatable results. The GIMP painting workspace remains flexible enough for sketches, concept art, and texture work.

In practice, GIMP works well for students, hobbyists, photographers, and designers who need a capable editor without a complicated setup. The interface can be rearranged, panels can be docked, and themes can be adjusted. GIMP photo editing sessions can stay lightweight on older hardware, while GIMP image editor projects can grow into multilayered documents. GIMP graphic design tasks benefit from grids, guides, and snapping, and GIMP retouching tools help polish final images before export.

A typical GIMP painting workspace includes brush presets, a color history, and layer groups. Artists can block in shapes, refine edges, and blend colors while keeping photo references nearby. GIMP photo editing and GIMP painting workspace features overlap in useful ways: a retoucher can paint on a mask, and a painter can use photo textures. GIMP image editor users often combine both approaches, and GIMP graphic design projects can reuse the same assets across formats.

## Starting with GIMP

Prerequisites: A computer that meets the compatibility table, a folder for projects, and a basic idea of what you want to edit or create.

1. **Prepare:** Close heavy background tasks and decide whether the session is for GIMP photo editing, GIMP graphic design, or practice.
2. **Configure:** Launch GIMP, choose a theme and panel layout, then set brush and color defaults for your GIMP painting workspace.
3. **Open:** Load an image or create a new canvas, then save the project in a native format before making major changes.
4. **Edit:** Use GIMP image editor tools such as layers, selections, and masks to build the result step by step.
5. **Retouch:** Apply GIMP retouching tools where needed, keeping brush size, hardness, and opacity under control.
6. **Export:** Save a copy in the format you need, then keep the layered source file for future GIMP photo editing sessions.
7. **Review:** Compare the export with the original, note what worked, and reuse those steps in the next GIMP graphic design task.
